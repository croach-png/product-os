## User Journeys

### Seller Journey

1. **List a bike** — Seller uploads photos, selects category (road, mountain, hybrid, e-bike), enters brand/model, and describes condition. Velo suggests a price based on comparable sales.
2. **Condition verification** — For bikes over £200, Velo arranges a video call inspection or the seller completes a 12-point self-assessment checklist. Bike gets a condition grade (A/B/C/D).
3. **Listing goes live** — Appears in search and category pages. Seller can boost with a premium listing (£15).
4. **Offer & acceptance** — Buyers can make offers or buy at asking price. Seller accepts/rejects/counters.
5. **Collection & payment** — Velo arranges courier pickup. Seller is paid 3 days after buyer confirms receipt.

**Key pain points:**
- Pricing is hard — sellers don't know what their bike is worth
- The condition checklist takes too long (~15 min) and causes 40% drop-off
- Payment delay (3 days) frustrates sellers used to instant payment on other platforms

### Buyer Journey

1. **Discovery** — Buyers find Velo via Google, social, or word of mouth. Land on homepage or category page.
2. **Browse & filter** — Search by type, brand, size, price, condition grade, location.
3. **Listing detail** — View photos, condition report, seller rating, price history.
4. **Purchase / offer** — Buy now or make an offer. Payment via card or Klarna (3 instalments).
5. **Delivery** — Bike delivered in 3-7 days. 48-hour inspection window to confirm or raise an issue.

**Key pain points:**
- Size uncertainty — "will this bike fit me?" is the #1 question to support
- Delivery takes too long compared to buying in a shop
- Photos are inconsistent quality — some sellers upload blurry phone pics

- **iOS App** — buyer browsing and purchase; seller listing via camera
- **Android App** — launched Q4 2025, feature parity with iOS
- **API** — used by 3 bike shop partners for bulk listing
