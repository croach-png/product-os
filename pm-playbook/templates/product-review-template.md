## Problem Alignment

### The Problem

Describe the problem (or opportunity) you're trying to solve. Why is it important to our users and our business? What insights are you operating on? And if relevant, what problems are you *not* intending to solve?

### JTBD

Outline the top 1–3 Jobs to Be Done.

1. [JTBD 1]
2. [JTBD 2]
3. [JTBD 3]

### High-level Approach

Describe briefly the approach you're taking to solve this problem. This should be enough for the reader to imagine possible solution directions and get a very rough sense of the scope of this project.

### Goals & Success

What does success look like? What metrics are you intending to move? Explain why these metrics are important if not obvious. Show the current metric, the size of the shift, and include a stretch goal where appropriate.

Include relevant information that gives you confidence to hit your target goal — e.g. success of similar work, pertinent external metrics ("X company reports Y% change with a similar approach").

Goals should ideally be translated into broader business impact, e.g.:

> "If we hit our target goal of X% [key conversion metric], this results in $X in incremental revenue and/or X incremental [key business outcome]."

Highlight any guardrails.

## Solution Alignment

### Key Features

Give an overview of what we're building. Provide an organised list of features, prioritised if relevant. Discuss what you're *not* building (or saving for a future release) if relevant.

### Key Flows

Show mocks/embeds of the experience. Link to any other documentation as necessary. Organise these around user journeys / use cases. Show enough of a clickthrough that people can walk away with a reasonable understanding of how the product works.

### Open Issues & Key Decisions

Keep track of open issues / key decisions here. Sometimes certain decisions are made that might feel controversial — document these here so people know the discussions have happened and there's strong awareness of the tradeoffs.
