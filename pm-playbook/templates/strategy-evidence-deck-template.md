# Context

The opening slides establish the landscape, where value concentrates, where headroom exists, what users are telling us, and where to focus. This section should answer: **why does this metric matter, what do users need, and where should we invest?**

## Slide 1: The Landscape

Set the scene with how the metric varies across segments. Use a table to show the spread — this could be a funnel breakdown, segment comparison, or step-by-step journey.

| Segment / Step | Key metric | Secondary metric | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Segment A | X | Y | [What's driving this] |
| Segment B | X | Y | [What's driving this] |
| Segment C | X | Y | [What's driving this] |

- Call out the biggest gaps or cliffs (e.g., "60% drop at step X", "20x difference between active and inactive")
- Highlight where performance is concentrated
- Bold the rows where the biggest losses or opportunities sit

# Template Notes

**When to use this template:** For strategy evidence decks that make the case for investing in a specific metric or outcome area. Best suited when you have a mix of research evidence and an opportunity tree to draw from.

**Structure principles:**
- **Context first** (slides 1-5): landscape, where value/volume concentrates, what users are saying, opportunity areas, revenue impact
- **Qual slide bridges data and strategy** — the audience should hear the user's voice before seeing where you'll invest. Customer-voiced headings are more compelling than analyst framing
- **Opportunity areas are the organising spine** — define them on Slide 4, thread them as category headers through the Opportunities & Barriers section, and map projects to them in Next Steps
- **Each opportunity slide**: qual evidence, quant evidence, opportunities — clearly separated
- **Category headers** in the Opportunities section should include "Investing now" and "Next on backlog" annotations so the audience always knows what's active vs planned
- **Summary**: distilled version of the full argument
- **Next steps**: active projects mapped to opportunity areas, separated by stage (active plan → in design → in discovery → backlog)

**Narrative consistency checklist:**
- Do the qual themes on the "What Users Are Telling Us" slide map to the opportunity areas on the "Where to Focus" slide?
- Do the opportunity areas on "Where to Focus" match the category headers in the Opportunities & Barriers section?
- Do the "Investing now" / "Next on backlog" annotations match the active experiments table in Next Steps?
- Does the Summary reflect the current state of the deck (not an earlier version)?
- Are data points (volumes, percentages, revenue figures) consistent everywhere they appear?

**Conventions:**
- Use your current ARPU (from data/funnel-context.md) for revenue calculations — always cite the source and date
- Flag assumptions with `> **⚠ To validate:**` callouts
- Reference study numbers (e.g., Study 0082) for all qualitative evidence
- Mark quantitative gaps explicitly as "Needs validation" rather than leaving them blank
- Use verbatim quotes where they exist — they're more compelling than summaries
- Keep opportunity lists as bullet points, not elaborated descriptions — the detail belongs in project docs
- Name opportunity areas as user outcomes, not internal labels
