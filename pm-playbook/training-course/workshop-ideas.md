# Workshop Output: Onboarding Invite Step Solutions

*This is a fabricated scenario for training purposes.*

---

## Workshop details

**Date:** Last Tuesday
**Attendees:** You (PM), Sam (TL), Priya (Designer), Jordan (Analyst), plus 2 engineers from the squad
**Format:** 90-minute structured brainstorm using HMW (How Might We) themes
**Problem statement:** How might we get more users to invite teammates during onboarding — or achieve the same activation outcome without requiring invites?

---

## HMW themes generated

1. HMW show the value of collaboration before asking for invites?
2. HMW reduce the friction of the invite action itself?
3. HMW let users experience team features without real teammates?
4. HMW make the invite feel like a benefit, not a chore?
5. HMW serve solo users while still encouraging team adoption?
6. HMW recover users who skip or abandon the invite step?

---

## Top solution ideas (grouped by theme)

### Theme 1: Show value before asking

**Idea A: Demo team workspace**
Pre-populate a sample workspace with 3 fictional teammates, sample tasks, comments, and activity. User experiences collaboration features with fake data before being asked to invite real people. After exploring, prompt: "Ready to bring your real team in?"

**Idea B: Video walkthrough of collaboration**
30-second video showing a real team using shared features — @mentions, real-time updates, shared dashboards. Plays automatically at the invite step. "This is what your team will see."

**Idea C: Success metrics preview**
Show the user a preview of their "Team Dashboard" populated with projected data: "Teams like yours typically save 5 hours/week on status updates." Makes the outcome tangible before the action.

### Theme 2: Reduce invite friction

**Idea D: Share-a-link instead of email entry**
Replace the email form with a shareable invite link. One click to copy, paste into Slack/email/Teams. Removes the "I don't know their email addresses" barrier.

**Idea E: Workspace URL as identity**
Let users create a workspace URL (company.taskflow.com) as the invite mechanism instead of individual emails. More natural for teams — "Just go to our workspace URL."

**Idea F: Calendar integration invite**
Connect to Google Calendar, detect frequent collaborators, suggest "Invite the 3 people you meet with most?" Auto-populated, one click to send.

### Theme 3: Simulate team features

**Idea G: AI teammate bot**
A bot teammate that responds to @mentions, completes sample tasks, and demonstrates collaboration features in real time. "Meet Alex, your demo teammate. Try @mentioning them in a task."

**Idea H: Template with built-in collaboration**
Offer a "Team Kickoff" template that includes pre-assigned tasks, comments, and a shared timeline. User experiences the structure of team collaboration through the template.

### Theme 4: Reframe the invite

**Idea I: "Get credit" positioning**
Frame the invite as benefiting the inviter: "Be the one who brought the best tool to your team. Inviting 3 people unlocks Premium features for your first month."

**Idea J: Progressive onboarding**
Remove the invite step from onboarding entirely. Instead, trigger invite prompts at natural collaboration moments — when the user creates their second project, when they try to assign a task, when they view the team dashboard.

### Theme 5: Serve solo users

**Idea K: Solo mode**
Detect solo intent (e.g. user skipped invite, hasn't mentioned a team) and switch to a personal productivity onboarding: "Looks like you're flying solo — here's how to get the most from your personal workspace." Invite prompts come later through in-product nudges.

### Theme 6: Recovery

**Idea L: Smart re-engagement**
For users who skip the invite step, trigger a contextual prompt after they've used the product for 3+ days: "You've created 12 tasks this week. Teams that use shared boards get 3x more done — ready to invite yours?"

## Workshop vote results

| Idea | Votes | Feasibility (TL) | Impact (Analyst est.) |
|------|-------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **J: Progressive onboarding** | 5 | Medium (2-3 weeks) | High — removes the gate entirely |
| **D: Share-a-link** | 4 | Easy (1 week) | Medium — reduces friction, doesn't address value gap |
| **A: Demo workspace** | 4 | Hard (4-6 weeks) | High — addresses the core value gap |
| **K: Solo mode** | 3 | Medium (2-3 weeks) | Medium — serves underserved segment |
| **L: Smart re-engagement** | 3 | Easy (1 week) | Medium — recovery for skip/abandon users |
| **I: Get credit positioning** | 2 | Easy (<1 week) | Low-medium — reframes but doesn't solve |

## Team observations

- Sam (TL): "Progressive onboarding is the biggest bang for buck. The invite step is the problem — remove the gate and the funnel opens up."
- Priya (Designer): "I'd combine the demo workspace with progressive onboarding. Let them see value first, then invite at a natural moment."
- Jordan (Analyst): "The data supports J + D. Users who come back to invite later have higher intent. And the link share removes a real friction point."
