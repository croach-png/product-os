*This is a fabricated scenario for training purposes. Competitor details are illustrative and may not reflect current real-world offerings.*

## Comparison: How competitors handle the invite step

| Feature | Our Product | Asana | Monday.com | ClickUp | Notion |
|---------|-----------|-------|------------|---------|--------|
| **Invite required?** | Yes (in flow) | No (deferred) | No (deferred) | No (optional) | No (deferred) |

## Key observations

### 1. Nobody else gates onboarding on invites

All four competitors let users reach value without inviting anyone. Team invites happen naturally after the user sees value, not as a prerequisite.

### 2. Demo/sample content bridges the value gap

Monday.com and ClickUp pre-populate workspaces with sample data so users can see what collaboration looks like before committing to it. This addresses the "I can't see the value" problem without requiring real teammates.

### 3. Time to first value varies dramatically

Our product takes ~8 minutes to reach any value (because the invite step blocks progress). Notion and ClickUp get users to value in under 2 minutes. This is a significant competitive disadvantage for acquisition-stage users who are comparing tools.

### 4. Solo use is a valid entry point

Notion's dominance in personal productivity shows that solo users can become team buyers over time. Asana's "My Tasks" view serves solo users who later bring in colleagues. Our current flow dismisses this path.

## Competitive implications

1. **Gate removal is table stakes** — we're the only product that blocks progress on team invites
2. **Demo content is the bridge** — showing collaboration value without requiring real invites
3. **Solo → team is a growth loop** — individual users who love the product invite colleagues naturally
4. **Speed matters** — the longer it takes to reach value, the more users we lose to faster competitors
